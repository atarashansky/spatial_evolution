# Main-text figure captions (Figs 1–9)

## Figure 1 | Does deleterious load freeze clone boundaries or fluidize them? It fluidizes them: takeover is 5–21× faster than neutral, and tissue-mapped parameters place four surveyed human contexts in the fluid regime — a model placement, not a measurement.

**(a)** The question. A renewing tissue is a mosaic of clones separated by interfaces; deleterious mutations (rate μ_d, cost s_d, load rate Λ = μ_d s_d) accrue in both clones. Equilibrium intuition holds that load acts like a surface energy that pins the boundary (top outcome, pale-blue pad); the alternative is that unequal decay of the two clones drives the boundary and fluidizes the mosaic (bottom outcome, pale-peach pad). Clone identity (grey/tan) is arbitrary and carries no fitness meaning; phase identity (frozen/fluid) is carried by the pale fills here and in (d).

**(b)** Raw clone-label fields at the same clock time (t = 250k frames; 768 × 256 strip, death–Birth spatial Moran model, periodic short axis), one representative run per condition. The neutral boundary (Λ = 0, grey frame) roughens without bound — fingers and detached enclaves; the loaded boundary (μ_d = 0.2, s_d = 0.01; Λ = 2×10⁻³, vermilion frame) is sharp on short scales because load suppresses short-wavelength copy-noise roughening (Supplementary Fig. S14) — not because it is under tension. Condition identity (grey = neutral, vermilion = loaded) threads to (c). The snapshot pair uses a 2× heavier load (Λ = 2×10⁻³) than the acceleration ensemble in (c) for visual clarity; the sharp phenotype holds across the fluid regime (Fig. 2).

**(c)** The result: takeover under load is faster, not slower. Median-fixation-time ratio T_fix(neutral) / T_fix(load) versus interface length L_short (strip 192 × L_short; Λ = 10⁻³, μ_d = 0.1, s_d = 0.01, with μ_b = 0 — no beneficial mutations in this arm). The acceleration grows from 5.0× at L_short = 16 to 21.2× [18.4, 24.3] at L_short = 256 (median 137.0k vs 2.91M frames; censored neutral runs enter as lower bounds, ≤6 of 150 per point) — larger interfaces accelerate most. n = 200 loaded and ~150 neutral runs per point; error bars, 95% bootstrap CIs of the median ratio; dashed grey line at 1 marks no effect.

**(d)** Where real tissues sit. The (Λ, s_d) plane classified by the freeze/flow criterion: a grid cell is frozen (pale blue) when its median fixation time is statistically indistinguishable from neutral (95% CI overlaps the neutral CI [485k, 691k] frames; neutral n = 200), fluid (pale peach) otherwise. Dots mark the 19 sampled cells (filled = frozen, open = fluid; n = 150–400 each); the dashed ridge is the ratchet criterion U_d/s_d ≈ 0.4 (Fig. 4), and pale washed territory beyond the sampled grid is extrapolation (dotted ridge). Horizontal bars: per-division load-rate ranges of four surveyed human tissue contexts (colonic crypt, sun-exposed epidermis, HSC/blood, hypermutator tumor) at their central per-mutation cost estimate (faint vertical whiskers span each tissue's tabulated s_d range); evaluated at the central estimates, all four lie in the fluid regime — the hypermutator tumor's upper s_d bound (3×10⁻²) approaches the extrapolated ridge at the low-Λ end of its range — takeover, not freezing, is the generic fate (Fig. 6 gives the full map with acceleration magnitudes).

Old panels (roughness growth and the σ_eff load-rate collapse) are in Supplementary Fig. S8. Data: `snapshot_fields.npz` (b), `tfix_results.parquet` medians via `panel_b_data.json` (c), `regime_map_summary.json` grid + `tissue_overlay_table.json` placements (d). All CIs bootstrap 95%.

## Figure 2 | Deleterious load accelerates fixation with no beneficial mutations, and the acceleration is a scaling law that grows with domain size.

**(a)** Median fixation time T_fix versus interface length L_short (log–log; strip 192 × L_short, death–Birth updating). Neutral (voter-class) boundaries slow with size as ~L^{0.66} [0.61, 0.71] (per-run power-law fit, n = 739), while loaded boundaries (μ_d = 0.1, s_d = 0.01, μ_b = 0; vermilion, n = 1,000) nearly stop responding to size, ~L^{0.16} [0.13, 0.19]; adding rare beneficial jackpots (μ_b = 10⁻⁵, orange, n = 1,000) gives ~L^{0.10} [0.07, 0.13] — a further factor of only 1.3–1.6. At L_short = 256 the loaded interface fixes 21× faster than neutral (137k vs 2.91M frames). Bands: bootstrap 95% CIs of the median (150–200 runs per point).
**(b)** The acceleration grows with transverse size: T_fix(load)/T_fix(neutral) falls from 0.20 to 0.046 (load) and 0.15 to 0.029 (load + jackpots) over L_short = 16 → 256, never approaching 1.
**(c)** Load dependence at fixed geometry (192 × 32): median T_fix falls smoothly and monotonically from 602k frames (Λ = 0) to 66k at Λ = 4×10⁻³ (s_d swept at μ_d = 0.1; 250 runs per point), with no threshold anywhere in the sampled range.
**(d)** Domain-length dependence at fixed L_short = 32 (filled) and 128 (open): neutral T_fix ∝ L_long^{1.73} [1.60, 1.90]; loaded (Λ = 10⁻³) T_fix ∝ L_long^{1.17} [1.11, 1.23] (150–200 runs per cell, zero censored at a 40M-frame cap).
**(e)** The loaded/neutral acceleration grows without saturation as L_long^{0.57} [0.42, 0.74], from 5.2× [4.5, 6.2] at L_long = 96 to 16.7× [12.8, 24.5] at L_long = 768 (L_short = 32), and is larger still at L_short = 128 (grey). Panel (f) of earlier versions (the T_fix collapse) is Supplementary Fig. S14. Data: `tfix_results.parquet` (a–c), `finite_size.parquet`, `finite_size_summary.json` (d–f); per-geometry gap statistics in Supplementary Fig. S9. All CIs bootstrap 95%.

## Figure 3 | The engine of acceleration: two ratchets clicking at different rates open the fitness gap as a random slope; the boundary microstructure adds a trench and a minor jackpot channel; the boundary-kinematic observable follows.

**(a)** Clone fitness versus signed distance from the interface, conditioned on arrest and resolved by arrest age. Arrested interfaces sit in a symmetric fitness trench — pooled over all arrests, −2.5% relative to same-time bulk at the profile minimum (95% CI [−2.8, −2.4]) — that deepens with time spent arrested: the |d| ≤ 2 mean goes from −2.4% for young arrests (<2.5k frames) to −4.0% beyond 25k frames. The trench is the microscopic root of the short-wavelength roughening suppression of Supplementary Fig. S14: protruding peninsulas are the most load-exposed sites and are pruned. Profiles are means over arrest windows; shaded bands, 95% CIs.
**(b)** Paired-passage test (n = 28,066 front passages, 512 × 64): comparing each site before and after a front sweeps it, the deposited wake is fitter than the tissue it replaced (paired difference +0.95%, run-clustered 95% CI [0.91, 0.99]%, n = 512 runs; passages are not independent within a run) yet still ~1.6% below bulk. Fronts advance into weak tissue and deposit a fitter — but still sub-bulk — wake; both channels build the trench.
**(c)** Interface displacement in single trajectories: long quiescent intervals punctuated by ballistic excursions (median displacement 31.5 rows [95% CI shown]) that terminate in arrest; ballistic segments in colour, arrests in grey.
**(d)** The engine: single-run clone log-fitness gaps g(t) = ln f_A − ln f_B fan out linearly in time — each clone's ratchet clicks at its own rate, so g grows as a random slope g ≈ ΔR·t and the ensemble spread grows as SD ∝ t (envelope), not the √t of a diffusing gap; inset, the gap variance grows super-linearly (Var ∝ t^χ, χ = 1.47–2.12 across geometries; Fig. 2d–f). Fixation (dots) is won by the clone whose ratchet clicked more slowly.
**(e)** Schematic of the two channels under one load: the primary engine is the steady random-slope drift of (d); when beneficial mutations are permitted, a boundary jackpot adds a ballistic leap (excursion) between arrests, contributing a further 1.3–1.6× (Fig. 2a). Arrested interfaces dig the trench of (a) while they wait.
**(f)** The falsification observable: mean-square displacement of the interface centre of mass h̄ versus time in divisions per site. Neutral (grey, μ_d = 0) and loaded (Λ = 10⁻³) interfaces are indistinguishable while zero-mode noise dominates and separate at the crossover t_x ≈ 177 divisions per site (r.m.s. excursion ~7 cells), beyond which the loaded boundary drifts ballistically (guide, MSD ∝ t²) while the neutral boundary continues to wander. This tracked-boundary signature — not the clone-size distribution — is what an experiment must record (Fig. 7).
Data: `scar_data.npz` (a, b), `excursion_stats.parquet` (c; 2,010 excursions), `panel_d_data.json` (d), `reduced_model_series.npz` (f). Diagnostics and the no-free-parameter test in Supplementary Fig. S10.


## Figure 4 | The freeze/flow boundary is Muller's ratchet-stall criterion, U_d/s_d ≈ 0.4 — not an effect-size threshold.

**(a)** Order parameter for the purging transition: winner-clone end fitness divided by the no-purging ratchet prediction exp(−Λ·(p_die/2)·t_fix) (p_die = 0.0274; the winner clone holds ~half the lattice), versus per-mutation cost s_d at fixed load rate Λ = μ_d·s_d = 10⁻³ (192 × 32, death–Birth updating; median of n = 150 runs per point, bootstrap 95% CIs). Below the threshold the ratchet runs freely and the prediction holds to within a factor ~3 (0.35× at s_d = 0.00125); above it, purging eliminates deleterious mutants inside their own clone before they fix locally, and the tissue holds 32× the predicted fitness at s_d = 0.04. The shaded band marks the purging onset, between s_d = 0.01 and 0.04. Note that this sweep at fixed Λ co-varies the rate (μ_d = Λ/s_d falls 128-fold across it); panels (d–f) separate the two variables.
**(b)** Median fixation time versus s_d at fixed Λ = 10⁻³ (tail ensemble, 192 × 32, n = 400 per point, zero censored; bars, bootstrap 95% CIs of the median). Purging starves the fitness-gap drift that drives fast fixation: T_fix rises from 82k frames at the drift optimum (s_d = 0.01) through 295k and 542k to 544k, saturating near but below the neutral (voter-class) median of 602k. The fraction of individual runs slower than the neutral median rises 0% → 14% → 42% → 46% across the four points.
**(c)** Survival functions (1 − CDF) of fixation time for the four s_d values in (b), log–log. Tail heaviness is non-monotonic in s_d: the Hill tail index α̂ (k = n/5, percentile bootstrap 95% CIs) falls from 3.15 [2.6, 3.8] in the drift regime to 2.49 [2.0, 3.0] at s_d = 0.04 and 2.60 [2.1, 3.2] at s_d = 0.08, then rises back to 2.95 [2.5, 3.8] at s_d = 0.16, where the extreme tail also recedes (p99 2.48M → 2.20M frames from s_d = 0.08 to 0.16). The tail index never approaches the α = 2 variance-divergence line at any (s_d, L_short) probed (9,200 runs; Supplementary Fig. S6), so fixation-time variance stays finite.
**(d)** Rate alone melts the frozen cell: holding s_d fixed and raising μ_d drives the fixation-time ratio T_fix(load)/T_fix(neutral) monotonically down — from 0.77 [0.63, 1.00] at μ_d = 0.006 to 0.093 [0.075, 0.113] at μ_d = 0.4 for s_d = 0.04, and from 0.79 [0.64, 0.98] to 0.16 [0.13, 0.19] over μ_d = 0.002 → 0.1 for s_d = 0.01 (150 runs per rung; neutral anchor 200 runs).
**(e)** The freeze/flow ridge in the (Λ, s*) plane: the threshold effect size s* (fixation-time ratio = 0.5, interpolated within s_d ladders) at eight load rates spanning 400× in Λ (filled circles, new low-Λ ladders; squares, the regime grid of Fig. 6), with the free fit s* ∝ Λ^{0.48 ± 0.03} (R² = 0.982). Model lines: Haigh U_d/s_d = const (γ = 1/2, solid; ΔAIC = −1.3, F-test p = 0.48 against the free fit — accepted), U_d/s_d² (γ = 1/3, dashed; ΔAIC = +12.5, p = 1.4×10⁻³ — rejected), and s* = const (γ = 0, dotted; ΔAIC = +30.2, F = 331, p = 1.8×10⁻⁶ — rejected). Along the ridge U_d/s_d = μ_d/s_d ≈ 0.4.
**(f)** The threshold is intensive over a sixteenfold range of clone area: the fixed-s_d rate crossing (s_d = 0.01), expressed as U_d/s_d = μ_d*/s_d, at N = 6,144 (192×32), 12,288 (192×64), 24,576 (192×128), 49,152 (192×256), and 98,304 (192×512) is 0.43 [0.32, 0.54], 0.46 [0.41, 0.52], 0.37 [0.23, 0.53], 0.39 [0.32, 0.53], and 0.51 [0.34, 0.67] (150 runs per rung and neutral anchor except the largest cell, 60 per rung — 58 at μ_d = 0.008, two planned runs not completed; zero frame-cap censoring and zero errors; bootstrap 95% CIs). Solid line and band, weighted fit against log₁₀N — slope −0.02 per decade [−0.22, +0.19], consistent with zero; dashed line, weighted mean U_d/s_d = 0.44 [0.40, 0.48]; the five points are homogeneous (χ² = 2.8, 4 d.f., p = 0.60). Filled markers, prior campaign (4× range); open markers, this extension. Data: `sdsweep_results.parquet` and `tail_ensemble.parquet` (a–c), `ridge_law.parquet` (d–f; 7,400 runs, zero censored). Fits and controls in Supplementary Fig. S11. Panel (f) additionally uses `intensivity_ext.parquet` / `intensivity_ext_summary.json` (the two new geometries). (g) Least-loaded-class census (192×32, s_d = 0.01, nine U_d/s_d rungs, 24 runs each; 192×128 at six rungs, 13–16 runs each; 312 runs): the mutation-free fraction f₀ of interfacial cells (within three columns of the boundary) versus U_d/s_d, identical on both geometries — f₀ = 0.81 (U_d/s_d = 0.1), 0.39 at the ridge (U_d/s_d ≈ 0.4), 0.003 (U_d/s_d = 5); f₀ = 0.5 at U_d/s_d = 0.29 [0.27, 0.30]. (h) Expected number of mutation-free cells among the eight sites contesting a boundary vacancy (8 f₀) and the probability that a clone's interfacial cells contain no member of the least-loaded class: 8 f₀ ≈ 3.1 at the ridge and crosses 1 only at U_d/s_d ≈ 0.79, where the absence probability crosses 0.5 (≈ 0.77) — the freeze/flow ridge is not the class's extinction, which lies inside the melted phase (data `n0_census.parquet`, `n0_census_raw.parquet`; kernel `strip_census.py`; run-level bootstrap CIs, 600 resamples). At fixed area the threshold is not shape-independent: across seven geometries at N = 24,576 and 49,152 it is flat for aspect ratio L_long/L_short ≤ 1.5 and rises for elongated strips (aspect ≥ 3), e.g. 0.39 [0.31, 0.52] (192×256) → 0.58 [0.47, 0.66] (384×128) → 0.68 [0.55, 0.89] (768×64) at N = 49,152 (pooled slope +0.16 [+0.04, +0.24] per decade of aspect, P = 0.003; ladders and the common-anchor decomposition in Supplementary Fig. S19).


## Figure 5 | The rule survives composition: broader distributions of fitness effects purge harder at matched load rate, because the ratchet criterion filters each mutation by its own effect — and every human tissue DFE lands in the fluid regime with purging active.

**(a)** Purging order parameter (winner mean fitness divided by the no-purging ratchet prediction exp(−Λ·divisions); 1 = free ratchet) versus DFE breadth, CV(s), at matched load rate Λ = μ_d·E[s] = 10⁻³ (E[s] = 0.01). Dots: bootstrap medians; whiskers: 95% CIs; n = 1,000 runs per condition on a 192 × 128 strip (walled y, deleterious-only, death–Birth updating, 20M-frame cap, zero censored). The order parameter rises monotonically from 6.25 [5.95, 6.64] (point mass) to 21.8 [20.0, 23.5] (lognormal σ = 1.5). Exponential (CV = 1.00) and lognormal σ = 1.0 (CV = 1.31) are statistically indistinguishable (median-difference 95% CI [−1.8, +0.6]): breadth, not distribution family, controls purging.
**(b)** Fixation probability per arising mutation by DFE decile (1 = weakest tenth of the distribution, 10 = strongest), log scale, same conditions as (a); shading orders conditions by breadth. The filter is monotone in every condition and steepens with breadth: weakest/strongest decile ratio 10× (lognormal σ = 0.5) → 708× (σ = 1.5; pooled across runs, run-level bootstrap 95% CI [544, 1015]). Every decile of every DFE — including the weakest — fixes below both the neutral anchor 1/N = 4.07×10⁻⁵ and the point-mass rate 1.42×10⁻⁵ (dashed lines): the criterion filters all of the DFE, per mutation, not merely its strong tail.
**(c)** Tissue-mapped DFEs: each tissue's measured (μ_d, E[s]) range midpoint (geometric mean; conversion in Methods and Supplementary Table 2) with an assumed lognormal σ = 1.0 shape — the in-vivo effect-size distribution shape is unmeasured. Dots: order-parameter bootstrap medians with 95% CIs; light band on colonic crypt: σ-sensitivity 0.5–1.5 (order parameter 1.35–1.69, verdict unchanged). All four tissues sit right of the no-purging line (order parameter 1.48–48.6) yet fix 9–17× faster than the neutral anchor (T_fix median 1.69M frames, n = 300, same geometry): purging is active in every tissue but nowhere strong enough to starve the drift engine — every tissue DFE is fluid; none is frozen.
n = 12,300 runs (6 ladder + 4 tissue + 2 colon-σ conditions at n = 1,000; neutral anchor n = 300), zero censored. Fixed-mutation decile counts use the min-over-surviving-cells proxy (upper bound on MRCA-fixed; consistent across conditions). The order parameter is geometry-dependent; all comparisons carry the same 192 × 128 geometry and its own point-mass anchor. Data: `production_dfe.parquet`; statistics: `summary_production.json`.

## Figure 6 | The regime map, and the two clocks: real renewing tissues occupy the fluid regime, and slow renewal buys calendar time, not a regime change.

**(a) The measurement.** Median clonal-boundary survival time relative to a mutation-free tissue, T_fix / T_fix^neutral, across the (Λ, s_d) plane at 192 × 32 (colour; n = 150–400 per cell, zero censored at the 30M-frame cap). Blue: boundaries collapse faster than neutral (fluid mosaic); red/white: boundaries persist as long or longer (frozen mosaic, where within-clone purging stalls the ratchet). The dashed contour (ratio = 0.5) is the freeze/flow ridge — the ratchet criterion U_d/s_d ≈ 0.4 of Fig. 4, a line of slope one in these coordinates (s* ∝ Λ^{1/2}); it bends through s_d = 0.04 at Λ ≈ 10⁻³. Hatched cell: requires μ_d > 0.8, not run.
**(b) Where real tissues sit.** Same plane, regions only. Horizontal lanes give order-of-magnitude Λ ranges for colonic crypt (~1–2 SNV/division), sun-exposed epidermis, and haematopoietic stem cells (~7–16 SNV/division; slow cyclers accumulate mutations by time, not replication), each computed as SNV/division × deleterious fraction (2–10%) × s_d over the passenger effect-size range; lane heights are for legibility only and carry no s_d information. The dotted violet line marks the ceiling of typical passenger effect sizes (s_d ≈ 10⁻²; the distribution extends below the mapped range). The dashed vermilion box places hypermutator tumours, whose per-division rates run 4–100× normal and whose copy-number-scale events extend s_d to ~3×10⁻². Arrows mark ranges extending past the plot. These are placements, not measurements — each spans 1–2 decades — but the conclusion is robust to the ranges: every placement sits at U_d/s_d of order 10–10³, one to three decades on the fluid side of the criterion, so every tissue placed lands in the fluid regime and none reaches the frozen corner. Sources and conversion assumptions in Methods and Supplementary Table 2.
**(c) The two clocks.** Divisions per site to takeover on the 192 × 128 lattice (a property of this lattice — fixation times are not intensive; Fig. 2d) and their conversion to calendar time by each tissue's renewal rate: ~5,100 divisions per site in colon and ~3,400 in blood, i.e. roughly a century or two of colonic renewal at 25–50 divisions per year and millennia of HSC renewal at ~1–2 divisions per year; epidermis and hypermutator tumours are shown in divisions only. A mosaic can be fluid in divisions and stable in decades.

## Figure 7 | Published clone-size distributions reject the strip kernel in every arm and cannot separate loaded from neutral dynamics; the discriminating observable is boundary kinematics, not clone size.

**(a)** Two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance between each control arm and the pooled kernel snapshot at the matched frame; filled symbols = raw kernel with mean-rescaled clone sizes (the manuscript's original construction, 48 runs), open symbols = kernel plus the fitted measurement layer of (b), tested in absolute µm² (error bars: 95% bootstrap CI, n_sim = 81k–598k measured clones). Grey region: the two-sample critical value at significance level 0.05; a kernel that reproduced the data would fall inside it. Every published control arm rejects both the neutral and the loaded kernel (D 2.7–14× critical raw; 2.7–12× after the layer); loaded and neutral curves are indistinguishable within the CI at every age, and the residual grows monotonically with age.

**(b)** Out-of-sample test of the measurement layer. The layer (induction thinning with adjacency fusion, per-clone lognormal projected footprint A = c·F with F ~ LogNormal(ln 59 µm² cell⁻¹, 1.06²), 1.29-µm² pixel rounding, 50.27-µm² detection floor) is fitted once, on the 1-month control arm only (KS = 0.026; footprint 59 µm² cell⁻¹ [95% CI 56.4–60.9], σ_F = 1.09 [1.06–1.13]), then frozen. Median (line) and 10–90% band of the layered neutral kernel versus the control data. The layered kernel matches at 1 and 3 months but coarsens as ⟨A⟩ ∝ t^0.80 (2D neutral drift), whereas the tissue median clone area saturates after 3 months (∝ t^0.31); at 12 months the predicted median (2,785 µm²) is 2.9× the observed (957 µm²). No static area transform can repair a mismatch in the time evolution: refitting the layer separately at each age still rejects at 12 months (D = 0.076, 2.8× critical) and would require the per-cell footprint to shrink 3-fold with age.

**(c)** Clones required per arm for 80% power to discriminate the loaded kernel from the neutral kernel, using the Neyman–Pearson likelihood-ratio test on the layered clone-area distributions (log-area kernel densities fitted on 30% of a 71k–600k-clone pool, Monte Carlo on the held-out 70%; test size verified 0.040–0.055; significance level 0.05). Hatched cells: 80% power is not reached at 51,200 clones per arm (the printed value is the maximum power reached there); power stays at or near the test's size in most cells and, where elevated (0.17–0.23 at 3 months for s_d ≤ 10⁻², 0.52 at s_d = 10⁻²/6 months), still falls far short of a designable experiment. 80% power is reached only at 12 months for s_d ≥ 5×10⁻³ (≈18,000 clones per arm at s_d = 5×10⁻³; ≈730 at s_d = 10⁻²). The manuscript's earlier 600–3,200-clone prescription and its kernel-vs-kernel KS "signal" of 0.04–0.09 are a mean-rescaling artifact on small integer clone sizes (see Methods): the rescaled KS is independent of s_d and equals the split-half self-null of the same neutral ensemble.

**(d)** The test the published data already permit. Purple: DEN (mutagenised) over control median clone area at each age (95% bootstrap CI); orange: the same ratio between the layered loaded and neutral kernels. The observed load contrast is a monotonically growing 9× enlargement (1-mo 0.84×, 3-mo 1.42×, 6-mo 3.40×, 12-mo 8.55×), whereas the kernel's deleterious-load channel produces at most 1.14× and only at 12 months — the wrong magnitude by ~50×. Consistently, the DEN 1-month arm gives an LR statistic +8.6 s.d. above the neutral null but also +9.4 s.d. above the loaded kernel's own prediction: it lies outside both members of the model family, so the test reports model inadequacy, not a discrimination in favour of load. The 3-month control arm (n = 4,152, above the prescribed 3,200) does not discriminate at s_d = 10⁻³ (z = 1.4, p = 0.087). The clone enlargement in mutagenised tissue is a driver-selection phenotype outside this kernel.
